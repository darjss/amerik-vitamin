What the customers actually did

The archaeology so far came from git and agent logs, which only show what got built. This page shows what happened on the other side of the screen: who came, from where, what they searched for, where they gave up, and which errors they ran into.

The whole story in one line

Unique visitors per day since launch. Every big event from the timeline is visible here. The ad launch spike came first, then the site went dark for two and a half weeks, then a long slow grind. When the site was set to noindex, traffic halved.

Money the browser saw

Server-side order_created events with $revenue, tracked since Jun 30. These are orders created, not orders paid. The paid number lives in the DB, and the manifesto says the DB only sees about 3% of the business anyway.

Facebook is the storefront

Since launch, 41% of visitors arrived inside the Facebook app's browser, and 87% were on a phone. Those visitors are the most likely to buy, and their payment hand-off is the most fragile part of the funnel.

In-app (FB/IG)Normal browser

In-app visitors added to cart 19.2% of the time, compared with 13.1% in normal browsers. They reached checkout 10.9% of the time, against 8.1%. The people coming from Facebook want to buy. The checkout loses them at the bank hand-off.

The bank app hand-off

A customer taps a bank, and the phone is supposed to open that bank's app. A "no hand-off" event means the app never opened. 42% of Khan bank taps never opened the app, and all 5 qPay wallet taps failed. This is the root of the repeated-orders bug, where a customer placed three orders of 1,121,000₮ in 110 seconds.

What people searched for

The search event fires as people type, so "ma", "mag", "magn" and "magne" are one person spelling magnesium. Inositol is the most searched term overall. People search in both Mongolian and English ("Витамин d", "Омега", "Магни").

Errors customers actually hit

    What the replays showed

    Replay Vision, PostHog's AI session watcher, summarized 21 recorded sessions between Aug 28 and Sep 3. Eleven went cleanly, mostly OTP logins and guest checkouts. Ten hit friction, and almost all of it sits between the cart, the order and the payment hand-off.
